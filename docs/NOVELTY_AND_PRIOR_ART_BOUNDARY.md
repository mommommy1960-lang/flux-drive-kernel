# Flux Drive / Aurora Novelty and Prior-Art Boundary

## Why this file exists

This project deliberately separates **new to this repository**, **potentially
distinctive integration**, **established prior art**, and **unverified new
physics**. A project can be valuable without pretending that every component is
novel.

This is an engineering-literature boundary, not a patentability opinion. A real
patent novelty/non-obviousness conclusion requires a dedicated patent search and
qualified legal review.

## Established prior art / known fields

The following ideas existed before this project and must not be claimed as
inventions merely because they appear in Aurora or Flux Drive:

- force and momentum conservation bookkeeping;
- photon momentum at the `P/c` scale and doubled normal momentum transfer for
  ideal reflection;
- torsion balances, pendulum balances, load cells, and multi-axis force/torque
  measurement for very small thrust claims;
- null runs, sham devices, reversed orientation, perpendicular orientation,
  thermal-drift controls, magnetic controls, and cable/feedthrough artifact
  testing;
- uncertainty propagation, expanded uncertainty, covariance treatment, and
  calibration traceability;
- hardware-in-the-loop simulation and digital twins;
- fail-safe / fail-closed interlocks and physical emergency disconnects;
- spacecraft fault detection, isolation, recovery, autonomous planning, and
  crew decision-support;
- model-based systems engineering for spacecraft;
- generation-ship / large self-sustaining spacecraft concepts;
- conventional electric, chemical, nuclear, solar-sail, and photon propulsion
  families;
- prior propellantless-thrust concepts such as EMDrive and Mach-effect devices.

High-precision SpaceDrive work at TU Dresden is especially relevant prior art
for the measurement problem: it developed sensitive thrust balances and found
that magnetic interactions, thermal effects, atmospheric effects, cable routing,
and orientation can create or expose false-positive thrust signatures.

## What has *not* been discovered here yet

As of this boundary document, the repository contains no calibrated physical
measurement demonstrating:

- reactionless propulsion;
- a net force without a measured external momentum channel;
- spacetime-curvature control;
- a warp bubble;
- a stable or traversable engineered wormhole;
- an engineered black hole suitable for transport;
- a city-sized Aurora spacecraft capable of flight.

Therefore there is presently **no new physical propulsion effect to claim**.

## Potentially distinctive integration contribution

What may be distinctive is the way this project combines several existing
engineering disciplines into one evidence-governed research architecture:

1. deterministic command/replay behavior;
2. fail-closed human authorization boundaries;
3. measured-channel ingestion rather than simulation-only output;
4. explicit force, energy, momentum, calibration, and uncertainty ledgers;
5. reaction-channel closure as a software-enforced gate;
6. reproducible raw-data and configuration retention;
7. preregistered falsification and artifact controls;
8. claim-state separation between simulation, prototype, measured anomaly,
   independently replicated effect, and verified propulsion;
9. human-governed autonomous-spacecraft software concepts linked to the same
   audit architecture;
10. a governance rule that no AI or automated subsystem gains authority merely
    because it is trusted, capable, persistent, or operationally central.

That combination can be a legitimate research/software contribution even if the
physical Flux Drive hypothesis is ultimately falsified.

## Aurora specifically

Autonomous spacecraft and fault management are not new. NASA and other space
programs have long developed autonomous planning, fault detection/isolation,
crew decision support, model-based systems engineering, hardware-in-the-loop
simulation, and digital twins.

Aurora's candidate contribution is therefore not "the first intelligent
spacecraft." The more defensible description is:

> a human-authorized, auditable autonomous vessel architecture that treats
> deterministic replay, explicit consent/authority boundaries, evidence-state
> labeling, and independently interruptible consequential actions as first-class
> engineering requirements.

Whether that integration is patentably novel is unresolved.

## Flux Drive specifically

The measurement philosophy is also not entirely new. Serious low-thrust
laboratories already use sensitive balances and artifact controls. The repository
can contribute by making those controls machine-readable, fail-closed, auditable,
and reproducible in an open software harness.

The physical mechanism remains a hypothesis. If a future controlled experiment
shows a repeatable force residual above the complete uncertainty budget, the
next question is not "Did we invent warp drive?" It is:

> Which known or unknown momentum-transfer mechanism explains the residual, and
> does the result survive independent replication?

Only after that question survives should novelty claims about physics begin.

## Practical novelty claim language

Safe present-tense language:

- "evidence-gated propulsion research platform";
- "auditable low-thrust measurement and falsification framework";
- "human-authorized autonomous vessel research architecture";
- "integrated deterministic replay, metrology, safety, and claim-boundary
  framework";
- "candidate integration contribution requiring independent prior-art review."

Avoid until proven:

- "invented reactionless propulsion";
- "working warp drive";
- "first sentient spacecraft";
- "proven wall phasing";
- "patented/patent-pending" unless a filing actually exists;
- "first-ever" without a documented search that supports it.

## Bottom line

We are **not yet on to a demonstrated new force or new propulsion physics**.
We may be on to a useful and potentially distinctive way to conduct ambitious
propulsion and autonomous-spacecraft research without allowing software,
excitement, or institutional pressure to outrun the evidence.
