# What tunneling and frequency matching actually mean

## The hand-through-wall statement

Quantum mechanics assigns a nonzero transmission probability to some
microscopic particles crossing a sufficiently narrow potential barrier. In a
simple rectangular-barrier approximation, the exponent has the form

\[
\log_{10} T \approx -\frac{2a}{\ln 10}\frac{\sqrt{2m\Delta E}}{\hbar}.
\]

The probability falls exponentially with barrier width and with the square
root of particle mass. A macroscopic object is not one particle: its atoms,
electrons, bonds, temperature, and environment would have to maintain a
coherent many-body state. The existing T-Sphere materials contain no method
for doing that.

## What frequency matching can do

Driving a system at a resonance can increase a particular transition or
oscillation. It does not make a solid wall and a robot cease interacting. A
wood plank does not have one “atomic frequency” that, when matched, removes all
electromagnetic, chemical, and exclusion interactions.

## Why becoming wood does not solve it

Changing a sphere into wood would be matter transmutation, not phasing. The
object would need the chemical composition and structure of wood, and wood
would still occupy space and interact with the plank. The sphere would also no
longer be the same engineered device unless its information and structure were
preserved by a separate, unproven process.

## Engineering conclusion

The tunneling idea is worth calculating as a falsifiable candidate, but the
calculation is a scaling screen, not a design recipe. The current branch now
contains that calculation and tests. It does not produce a usable macroscopic
transit mechanism.
